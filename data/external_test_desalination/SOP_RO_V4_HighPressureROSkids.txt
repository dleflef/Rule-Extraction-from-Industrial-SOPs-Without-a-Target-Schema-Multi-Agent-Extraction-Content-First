RO-SOP-V4: Standard Operating Procedure for High-Pressure Reverse Osmosis Skids

1. Purpose and Scope
This document outlines the standard operating procedures for the primary high-
pressure reverse osmosis (RO) membrane skids. It applies to all shifts and
operating personnel at the Seawater Reverse Osmosis facility. The guidelines
herein ensure maximum membrane lifespan and guarantee permeate water quality
meets municipal standards. Management reserves the right to amend this text
based on seasonal feedwater variations. The facility operates continuously,
processing millions of gallons of feed water daily to supply the local
municipality. This manual supersedes all previous versions distributed
before the current calendar year.

2. System Overview and Technical Background
The desalination process at this facility utilizes thin-film composite
membranes designed specifically for seawater applications. When seawater
is pressurized against the semi-permeable membrane surface, pure water
molecules pass through the microscopic pores while larger salt ions are
rejected. This phenomenon, known as reverse osmosis, naturally opposes
the osmotic pressure of the concentrated salt solution.

Several physical phenomena occur during normal operation. Concentration
polarization refers to the accumulation of rejected salts at the membrane
boundary layer. This localized increase in salinity raises the effective
osmotic pressure, thereby decreasing the net driving pressure available
for permeate production. The plant employs cross-flow filtration to
mitigate this effect, meaning that the feed water sweeps tangentially
across the membrane surface. A portion of the feed water continuously
washes the rejected salts out of the membrane vessel as brine or concentrate.

The physical infrastructure includes high-pressure centrifugal pumps,
energy recovery devices, chemical dosing skids, and precision instrumentation.
Energy recovery is achieved through isobaric pressure exchangers, which
transfer the hydraulic energy of the high-pressure brine directly to
incoming feed water. This dramatically reduces the electrical load on
the primary intake pumps, enhancing overall plant efficiency.

Temperature fluctuations in the raw seawater significantly influence
membrane performance. Warmer water possesses lower viscosity, increasing
the permeate flux rate at a given pressure. Conversely, colder water
during winter months inherently necessitates higher feed pressures to
maintain the exact same volumetric production rate.

3. General Startup Constraints
Commencing production necessitates careful sequencing to protect the
fragile membrane structures from mechanical shock. The sequence operates
automatically, although operator observation of the flow meters is
highly recommended during the initialization phases.

[OP-ST-001] The high-pressure intake pump must ramp up at a maximum rate of 0.5 bar per minute to prevent membrane telescoping.
[OP-ST-002] The anti-scalant dosing pump must be energized and verified active prior to raw water valve actuation.
[OP-ST-003] During startup, the permeate bypass valve must remain fully open, and the brine reject flow must be logged every 10 minutes.
[OP-SD-001] The main feed breaker must be tripped immediately if catastrophic seal failure occurs.
[OP-ST-004] If permeate conductivity fails to drop below baseline within 20 minutes, immediately execute the shutdown sequence mandated by [OP-SD-001].

4. Chemical Dosing Protocols
Pre-treatment chemistry functions to keep scaling minerals, such as
calcium carbonate and barium sulfate, dissolved in the concentrate stream.
Anti-scalants disrupt crystal formation at a molecular level. Sodium
bisulfite (SMBS) removes residual chlorine that would otherwise oxidize
and permanently destroy the sensitive polyamide membrane layer.

Operators must wear nitrile gloves and splash goggles when handling sodium bisulfite.
The dosing skid containment bund must be pumped empty within 4 hours of any detected spill.
All empty chemical carboys require a triple rinse with permeate water before disposal.

Handling bulk chemicals involves inherent environmental and safety risks
due to local acidity or basicity. The physical layout of the chemical
building isolates oxidants from reducing agents to prevent any accidental
cross-contamination. Transfer piping utilizes specialized fluoropolymers
highly resistant to corrosive attack over decades of continuous service.

5. Operational Setpoints and Thresholds
Hydraulic parameters are continuously logged by the central SCADA system
at one-second intervals. Variations in baseline pressure often indicate
the initial onset of membrane fouling or inorganic scaling.

[OP-AL-001] Membrane Feed Pressure requires strict monitoring against a target of 55.0 bar. A low-tier alert triggers at 52.0 bar, while a critical low trip occurs at 50.0 bar. A high-tier alert sounds at 58.0 bar, and a critical high trip forces shutdown at 60.0 bar.

The differential pressure across the membrane array also provides insight
into the physical fouling status. While individual vessels differ slightly
in their flow characteristics, the bulk average serves as a reliable
indicator of optimal cleaning intervals.

7. Personnel Access and Approvals
Safety protocols map human presence in the high-pressure zone to
specific operational states. High-pressure piping poses significant
kinetic energy hazards in the highly unlikely event of a mechanical rupture.
Authorization requirements for the high-pressure skid zone vary by
operational phase and staff role.

The internal zoning system maps directly to proximity sensors on the main
access gates. Electronic badge sweeps record entry and exit times for
compliance auditing by the safety management team.

8. Environmental Considerations
Brine discharge represents the primary environmental interaction for
the desalination facility. The concentrated reject stream contains
approximately twice the salinity of the raw intake water. Specialized
diffusers located offshore ensure rapid mixing with the surrounding
currents, minimizing any localized osmotic impact on marine life.
The discharge plume is modeled continuously based on tidal patterns
and seasonal oceanographic variations.

9. Maintenance Philosophy
Predictive maintenance practices form the core of the asset management
strategy. Vibration analysis on rotating equipment, thermography of
electrical switchgear, and acoustic monitoring of check valves provide
early warning signs of component degradation. By relying on condition-
based interventions rather than strictly calendar-based overhauls,
the facility maximizes uptime and minimizes unnecessary replacement
of healthy mechanical seals and bearings.

10. Data Logging and Archival
All process variable trends, alarm histories, and operator actions are
historized in a redundant server cluster located in the central control
room. This data repository supports long-term performance normalization,
allowing process engineers to track membrane permeability and salt rejection
decline over the typical five-to-seven-year membrane lifespan. Furthermore,
regulatory compliance necessitates the retention of all water quality data
for a period spanning ten years. The archiving software utilizes a lossless
compression algorithm to store the high-frequency telemetry efficiently.

11. Operational Troubleshooting and Diagnostics
When permeability declines unexpectedly, process engineers conduct
membrane autopsies on sacrificial elements. Destructive testing reveals
whether the foulant is primarily biological slime, organic colloids, or
inorganic scale. This structural analysis feeds directly back into the
design of the pretreatment chemicals, optimizing the coagulant blend.
